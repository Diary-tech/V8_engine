<section className="content-section intro-section">
  <SectionHeading eyebrow="INTRODUCTION" title="The crankshaft in 60 seconds" />
  <div className="prose-grid">
    <p>A four-stroke V8 completes one full 720° combustion cycle across its eight cylinders. That means eight firing events, spaced at 90° of crank rotation in both cross-plane and flat-plane layouts.</p>
    <p>The crankshaft geometry does not change that global 90° interval. It changes which cylinder bank produces each event and how the crank's rotating and reciprocating masses are arranged.</p>
  </div>
  <Callout>
    <strong>Key correction:</strong> do not describe cross-plane V8s as globally “uneven firing.” The firing interval is even. The unevenness discussed here is the <strong>alternation between banks</strong>.
  </Callout>
</section>

<section className="content-section">
  <SectionHeading eyebrow="SOUND" title="Why the sound differs" />
  <p>The crank layout is a major factor because it changes the sequence in which each bank's exhaust pulses arrive. A flat-plane V8 can present evenly spaced pulses to each bank, while a cross-plane arrangement can group pulses on a bank before a longer gap.</p>
  <p>That is only part of the sound. Exhaust design also matters: manifold length and geometry, collector arrangement, and systems such as 4-1 or 4-2-1 layouts reshape the pressure waves before they reach the tailpipe.</p>
</section>

<section className="content-section">
  <SectionHeading eyebrow="APPLICATION" title="Real-world use" />
  <div className="prose-grid">
    <p>Cross-plane crankshafts are common where smooth primary balance, broad road use and manageable NVH are important. The additional crank mass and exhaust-pulse grouping are accepted as part of the architecture.</p>
    <p>Flat-plane crankshafts are associated with high-performance V8 applications where lower rotating inertia and evenly alternating bank pulses can support the desired engine response and exhaust behavior. Second-order vibration is a design consideration handled through the engine structure and mounts.</p>
  </div>
</section>
